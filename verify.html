FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is a Japanese auction sheet?

It is an inspection report made at a Japanese vehicle auction, typically showing the grade, recorded mileage, vehicle condition diagram and inspector notes. It describes the vehicle at that time, not necessarily today.

Why should I verify the auction sheet before buying?

A photo of an auction sheet alone does not prove it belongs to the car being sold. Checking chassis and auction details against available records can reveal inconsistencies. Always arrange an independent physical inspection too.

Which auction sheets can Kansai review?

Our service is for buyers in Malaysia and covers Japanese auction sheets from the last 5 years. We need a readable chassis number and lot number. The auction date is optional when submitting the request.

What does the verification check — and what can't it guarantee?

We review available chassis, lot and auction information and report what we can or cannot confirm. A check cannot guarantee the car's current mechanical condition or detect damage that occurred after the auction. Missing records do not automatically mean a sheet is fake.

How do I submit my auction sheet?

Enter the chassis number, lot number and WhatsApp contact in the form above. Add the auction date if known. Continue to WhatsApp and attach a clear photo or PDF of the entire sheet.

Where are the chassis and lot numbers?

They are usually near the top or in the vehicle details block. Use the annotated example above to identify them; layouts vary by auction house.

Can I check a car sold by another dealer?

Yes. You can request a check before paying for a vehicle, even if it is being sold by another dealer.

Do I have to visit the KL office?

No. Requests are handled through WhatsApp for customers across Malaysia. Our KL office is at 3 Towers, Jalan Ampang.
